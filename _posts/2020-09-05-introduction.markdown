<p>Hello. </p><p><span><span style="font-size:medium;"><b>What is this?</b></span></span></p><p><span>This is a blog on my ongoing journey towards a researcher in UAVs and machine learning . It will cover a range of topics from computer engineering, optimization, reinforcement learning, </span>UAVs, aircraft design, swarming,  and of course my 2 cents on all of it. (quite a mix, ain\'t it?)</p><p><b><span style="font-size:medium;">Why here?</span></b></p><p>I\'ll be honest. I wanted to start this blog over at medium.com. There were two primary reasons that stopped me.</p><p>1. I felt the blogs there are too pretty. It goes like learn &gt; code(copy) &gt; run &gt; ACHIEVE. This is far from the truth. So I wanted to keep things more candid. A bit informal. I feel this helps in gaining more insight into the writers\' thought process.</p><p>2. Supplementing the 1st reason, I wanted a more secluded private corner of the internet where I could yap away. blogspot is good in this regard. The bad functionality and poor GUI pays off. No offense of course. </p><p>Still, it is almost a sort of convention now for machine learning/computer science practitioners to write at medium. </p><p>But I am neither one of those things. I am a Mechanical engineer. Yep.</p><p><span style="font-size:medium;"><b>Who am I(again)?</b></span></p><p>I am a Mechanical engineering graduate from Delhi Technological University, Delhi, India. I get it, the mechanical title feels a bit out of place. Me too. I wanted to become a mechanical engineer for as long as I can remember. And I did. It was my first choice. So how am I here?</p><p>I was properly exposed to programming as an extra subject in my senior secondary year at school and I loved every bit of it. But it still didn\'t strike me as a career choice. I wasn\'t particularly very good at mathematics as well so computer science always struck me as an abstraction which maybe just wasn\'t for me and consequently I never tried to venture into that dense dense forest. </p><p>Come to college, I joined this technical team of spirited individuals who focussed on developing unmanned aerial vehicles (or drones) for various projects and competitions. Joining and being selected in this team was one of the best things that had happened as it widened a lot of horizons. Being a multidisciplinary team of students from all branches, It was here that I was fully exposed to the expanse of aerospace engineering computer science. While not directly involved in the software tasks, I always picked up what I could and tried my best to fuse some of the knowledge of both mine(mechanical) and their departments(software). My competent nature was a perfect fit for the team and this, in turn, allowed me to grow beyond what I had envisioned my bachelor\'s degree to be. </p><p>The curriculum taught in the mechanical classes was outdated and the orthodox thinking of most of the teachers was detrimental to my initial aspirations which were molded accordingly. I worked for three years in the team as a UAV designer and always yearned to build better designs through computational techniques. This in turn opened the door to mathematical optimization and machine learning. The genetic algorithms and particle swarm optimizers. There was so much to learn. As I progressed in 4th year, I began to love maths and started to think with an open mind. A few relevant electives helped a lot along the way. My interests were(are) now UAVs, aerospace, optimization, machine learning, and nature-inspired algorithms. My goals were now much more general. </p><p style="text-align:left;"><span style="text-align:center;"></span></p><blockquote><span style="font-size:large;font-style:italic;"><a></a></span><span></span><span style="font-size:small;"></span>I want to be of maximum help to humanity and solve problems using AI and UAVs </blockquote><p></p><p><span style="font-size:medium;"><span style="text-align:center;"></span></span></p><blockquote><p></p></blockquote><p style="text-align:left;"><span style="font-size:medium;"><b>What now?</b></span></p><p>The UAV part I had worked on a lot in the past years and was fairly good at it. I had to work extensively on the AI part. It is extremely daunting to start raw in this field because there is so much development every day and a LOT of smart people already working on it. It is overwhelming and hard. There aren\'t many dividends at all at the initial stages.</p><p>But the important thing is to stick to it. Defining a goal such as above is not a bad thing to have while you\'re at it. I am here one year later and much better than last year. Much better than yesterday.</p><p>But more on what I am working on/ worked on recently in the next posts.</p><p>I\'ll try to keep a casual structure and not too many fancy words. Bye for now.<br />Thanks for reading!</p>
