<p>&nbsp;What comes to your mind when you read the words "Decentralized drone swarms"? Is it a view of multiple drones
    being used for mass destruction at enemy lines or perhaps an even more dystopian environment akin to skynet where
    aerial robots talk to each other, monitor our every movement and issue instructions for <i>human</i> slaves?</p>
<p>I won't deny the over-exaggeration of the above scenarios, nor will I say that its impossible ;)</p>
<p>But for me, the first thing that comes to mind is the picture below.&nbsp;</p>
<p><img alt="Starlings: Mapping and modelling the ballet of the skies - BBC News" class="n3VNCb" data-noaft="1"
        src="https://news.bbcimg.co.uk/media/images/78499000/jpg/_78499108_murmuration1.jpg"
        style="height: 443.607px; margin: 0px; width: 820px;" title="Starling murmurations" /></p>
<p>The sheer art of it.</p>
<p>The mere possibility and opportunity at our disposable to emulate a breathtaking system like that into reality with
    machines made out of silicon is wondrous to me. I often get asked this question (mostly by oblivious people in
    fields related to art/design).</p>
<p>How can you spend the entire day looking at lines of code on hollow black screens?</p>
<p>They are right from their perspective. It does <i>look&nbsp; </i>hollow sometimes. And when there is only one right
    way to do something, it gets frustrating. But behind the that <i>hollow&nbsp; </i>terminal is a tenacious motive. A
    motive to make something unreal and advance the human race to the best of our abilities. It is remarkable how people
    with extremely diverse cultures and opposing mindsets can come together to solve the most crucial problems by just
    writing lines of code and watching hollow black screens :). A feat very similar to what the starling birds do in the
    picture above and which goes unnoticed at the individual scale but only becomes overwhelming when you zoom out and
    look at the expanse of it.</p>
<p>The above picture and opportunity gets my blood running. How far can the human race push itself to get the closest to
    developing a robust system like that? If by its nature,&nbsp; evolution doesn't stop, will there be even more
    complex natural systems by the time we reach close to developing the ones today? Also, who<i> created</i> this?</p>
<p>Okay maybe shouldn't have asked the last one. But note that the emphasis is on "created" and not "who" :} Because
    both trying to make something similar out of generations of collaborative knowledge and extracted man-made materials
    seems like a field of art itself.</p>
<p>Anyway. I love such musings.</p>
<p>I had been working towards something similar(though not even remotely close to the picture) in my job/fellowship and
    thought I'd share it over here. I'm thinking of blogging bits of my steps throughout the way, but don't know if I'll
    be religious enough. Also, there is the issue of putting up information before publishing papers. </p>
